# Exposed decision tables: how many are real risks?

Generated by `run_dmn_exposed_sample.py` (`latc_check/dmn_exposed_sample.py`) from `results/corpus/results.json` and the hand classification in `results/corpus/exposed_sample_classes.json`.

## Frame and sample

`SUMMARY.md` counts 369 exposed tables over distinct files (single-hit, no catch-all rule, an undeclared string input whose entries mention a string literal). Many are copies of one table content; the sampling frame has one entry per distinct table content (the `fingerprint` of `dmn_corpus`): 190 tables. A simple random sample of 50 was drawn with `random.Random(20210101).sample` over the frame sorted by fingerprint. `exposed_sample.csv` lists the sample with the exposed input column(s), the literals the rules mention there, the hit policy, the number of rules, and whether some rule has `-` in the column.

## Classes

* **OPEN**: the input's real-world domain is open or larger than the values the rules mention, so an unmentioned value can plausibly arrive and reach no rule.
* **CLOSED**: the domain is closed by convention or by the upstream model (yes/no, a fixed status set the rules enumerate, an enumeration fixed elsewhere), so the risk is low.
* **PARTIAL**: the table is deliberately partial or a test fixture (a no-match test, a tutorial fragment, an analyser fixture with intentional gaps).
* **UNCLEAR**: cannot be judged from the model.

Each table was read in its model file (column label, literals, outputs, names and documentation nearby). When the choice between OPEN and CLOSED was doubtful, the table was recorded as UNCLEAR.

## Domain of the column, fixtures included

A second judgement records, for every sampled table, what the exposed column's domain would be if the table were used for real, fixtures included:

* **OPEN**: open or larger than the mentioned values in the real world; 7 of 50 (Wilson 95%: 7% to 26%).
* **CLOSED**: closed by convention or by the upstream model; 10 of 50 (Wilson 95%: 11% to 33%).
* **COVERED**: the column's entries already match every value through `not(...)` or expressions, so nothing is missed; 5 of 50 (Wilson 95%: 4% to 21%).
* **SYNTHETIC**: placeholder values with no real-world domain, as in syntax and engine tests; 24 of 50 (Wilson 95%: 35% to 61%).
* **UNCLEAR**: cannot be judged from the model; 4 of 50 (Wilson 95%: 3% to 19%).

The 5 COVERED tables are counted as exposed by the measure although every value reaches some rule: the literal count includes values inside `not(...)` and expressions.

## Estimate

OPEN share: 1/50 = 2% (Wilson 95%: 0% to 10%).

* Over the 190 distinct exposed tables: about 4 at real risk (Wilson range 1 to 20).
* Over the 369 exposed tables counted over distinct files, applying the same share: about 7 (range 1 to 39). Weighting each sampled table by its number of distinct-file copies instead gives an OPEN share of 7/79 copies = 9%, about 33 tables (a ratio estimate; copies are unevenly spread, so its interval is wider than the Wilson interval above; here one OPEN table with 7 copies drives it).

This is an estimate from one rater, reading each model without its authors' context. The sampling unit is the distinct table content; the classes were assigned to that content, and copies in other files inherit them.
